# Preface

Inkframe is a free, open-source 2D animation studio. It draws and paints, rigs characters with bones, animates with keyframes or frame by frame, and exports to video, the web and game engines. If you have used Adobe Animate, Toon Boom Harmony, Moho or Photoshop, much of it will feel familiar on purpose: the tools sit where you expect them, and their shortcuts are the ones your fingers already know.

This book is a guided tour of Inkframe for artists. It doesn't assume you have animated before, only that you like to draw. Each chapter teaches a group of tools by using them, with screenshots of the real app beside every step, and ends with something that moves.

## Who this book is for

This book is for illustrators, cartoonists and students who want to make their drawings move, and for animators coming from another program who want to find their way around Inkframe quickly. You won't need to write any code; the few places where Inkframe can be scripted are mentioned in passing, for later.

## What this book covers

*Chapter 1, Meet Inkframe*, tours the window: the toolbar, the stage, the timeline and the panels, how to rearrange them, and how to find any command by typing what you want.

*Chapter 2, Drawing with Vectors*, covers the shape tools, the Pen and the Pencil, reshaping and combining shapes, gradients and line styles, brushes and line textures, and the guides that help you draw straight.

*Chapter 3, Painting and Colour*, turns to pixels: brushes, fills and retouching tools, selections and adjustments, Harmony-style ink and paint, palettes, and tracing a painting into vectors.

*Chapter 4, Your First Animation*, bounces a ball. Along the way you'll meet keyframes, easing, motion paths, onion skins, the value graph and motion presets.

*Chapter 5, Frame by Frame*, is about hand-drawn animation: drawings that swap from frame to frame, inbetweens, shape tweens, the light table, the exposure sheet and lip sync.

*Chapter 6, Symbols, the Library and Organising*, shows how to reuse artwork as symbols, keep it in the Library, swap one for another, and tidy layers into folders and templates.

*Chapter 7, Rigging a Character*, builds puppets: parented parts posed with the Bone tool, then Moho-style skeletons that bend whole drawings, with rig setup, the Bones panel and target bones.

*Chapter 8, Smart Bones, Corrections and Dynamics*, makes those rigs clever: bones that switch drawings and reshape them as they turn, hand-drawn corrections, limits, stretch, and hair that swings by itself.

*Chapter 9, The Camera, Depth and Effects*, moves the camera through a multiplane scene, then adds filters, colour effects, motion blur and particles.

*Chapter 10, Exporting Your Work*, gets your animation out as video, image sequences, sprite sheets, Lottie and interactive web pages, one project or many at a time.

*Appendix A* lists every tool and its key, and *Appendix B* every command and where to find it.

## To get the most out of this book

Download Inkframe for macOS, Windows or Linux from its releases page, github.com/WhateverTrevor/inkframe/releases, and install it. A pen tablet is welcome but not needed: everything in this book can be done with a mouse.

Inkframe comes with example projects (**File › Open Example…**). Several chapters start from one, so you can follow along with exactly what the screenshots show.

> Tip: Every screenshot in this book is taken from the app itself each time the book is built, so what you see here is what you'll see on your screen.

## Conventions used

There are a few conventions used throughout this book.

**Bold**: a menu, a button, a panel or anything else you see on the screen, such as **Modify › New Skeleton**. The › points into a menu.

Keys to press look like this: [[⌘K]]. Shortcuts are written for a Mac; on Windows and Linux, use [[Ctrl]] for [[⌘]] and [[Alt]] for [[⌥]]. [[⇧]] is the Shift key.

`Code font`: a file name or something to type.
